What the chart measures

A classical cost curve for AI operations

Each block’s width is the location’s commissioned design IT power in MW. Its height is the electricity cost of performing 1019 dense-BF16 floating-point operations. Blocks run from the lowest cost on the left to the highest on the right.

Read $0.20 on the y-axis as:

At the stated electricity price, PUE and technology, the electricity required for 10 quintillion dense-BF16 operations costs 20 US cents. The inverse—5 × 1019 FLOPs per dollar—is also reported in the downloadable data.

Cost calculation

One common conversion

All prices are converted with 2025 Federal Reserve annual-average FX. Tariff demand charges use 730 hours per month and a 90% load factor. Common-technology PUE is fixed at 1.20.

Provincial MW = rectangle area ÷ full treemap area × 10,430,000 racks × 2.5 kW ÷ 1,000 Tariff $/kWh = [energy charge + demand charge ÷ (730 × 90%)] ÷ annual FX Cost per 10¹⁹ FLOPs = facility kW × $/kWh ÷ (dense FLOPs/s × 3,600) × 10¹⁹ × technology multiplier

Audit corrections

Three material weaknesses removed

These are not cosmetic changes: each one alters either a block width, a price claim or the evidential meaning of a curve.

March matches March

The CAICT provincial treemap is now scaled to 10.43 million racks at 31 March 2025. The later June national total is not mixed into a March distribution.

442.5 MW, not 269.4

Hohhot width is commissioned design IT capacity: 11 × 16,091 racks × 2.5 kW. The reported 326 MW reconciles as occupied facility load after utilization and PUE.

No invented weighting

The unsupported 169 MW Guangdong regional weighting is gone. The Pearl River Delta tariff is applied uniformly and explicitly labelled a conservative provincial proxy.
