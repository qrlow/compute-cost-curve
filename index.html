How to read it

The first curve values cheap power. The second tests whether chip access overwhelms it.

The first curve is the clean electricity-price experiment: every block uses 180 dense-BF16 PFLOP/s, 120 kW rack power and PUE 1.20. Because the technology is identical, cheaper electricity always appears farther left.

The second is a domestic-frontier counterfactual. Current BIS rules permit H200-class exports to approved China customers only case by case; they do not make a GB200 NVL72 generally available. The scenario therefore assigns CloudMatrix384 to China and GB200 to the non-China benchmarks. This is an analytical assumption, not legal advice or a claim about every installed accelerator.

Electricity $ per 10¹⁹ dense-BF16 FLOPs
  = (120 kW × 1.20 PUE × electricity $/kWh)
    / (180 PFLOP/s × 3,600) × 10¹⁹

Constrained China cost
  = same-location GB200 cost × 2.5

2.5 ≈ 4.1× system power / 1.67× dense-BF16 compute

Both curves use peak dense-BF16 specifications rather than measured useful model output. Construction, financing, hardware purchase price, utilization and model quality remain excluded.

Observation rule

The reference date is 31 December 2025. A displayed value must describe a period ending on or before that date; its source timing or normalized period end remains visible.

Cost denominator

Tariff blocks are transparent counterfactual bills for a large, steady high-voltage load. They are not claimed hyperscaler contracts.

Remaining uncertainty

The CloudMatrix power adjustment is estimated rather than a primary full-system measurement. The other 21 Chinese provinces and most global markets are also omitted.
